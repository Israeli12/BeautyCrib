How to choose a face cleanser for your skin type

Your cleanser is the only product your skin meets twice a day. It deserves more thought than it usually gets.

The Beauty Crib Editors 21 August 2026 7 minute read

Most people inherit their cleanser. It was on a shelf, it smelled good, the foam was satisfying. Then they spend money on serums trying to fix the dryness or congestion that the cleanser is quietly causing.

Start instead with one question: how does your skin feel fifteen minutes after washing? Not immediately, when everything feels fresh, but a quarter of an hour later. Comfortable means the cleanser fits. Tight, itchy or shiny within the hour means it does not.

Dry and sensitive skin

Look for cream, milk or a low-foam wash built around hydrating and barrier-supporting ingredients such as hyaluronic acid, panthenol, ceramides or centella. Avoid high-foam formulas that rely on strong surfactants, and avoid anything that lists alcohol high in the ingredients.

Oily and combination skin

A gel or foam with a mild exfoliating acid keeps pores clear without the rebound oiliness of a harsh wash. Salicylic acid is oil-soluble, which is why it reaches inside the pore rather than working only on the surface. If only your T-zone is oily, treat that zone rather than the whole face.

Acne-prone skin

Two active routes are worth knowing. Salicylic acid clears the pore lining, and benzoyl peroxide works on the bacteria involved in breakouts. Benzoyl peroxide is stronger and drier, so introduce it slowly, use it as a short-contact wash, and expect it to bleach coloured towels and bedding.

Whichever you choose, one active cleanser is enough. Stacking an acid wash, an acid toner and a strong treatment is the most common route to a damaged barrier, which looks confusingly similar to the problem you were trying to solve.

Normal skin, and everyone in between

If your skin is largely uncomplicated, use that freedom on texture and tone rather than on damage control. A niacinamide foam or an enzyme powder gives a gentle polish and keeps skin looking even without asking much of it.

Four signs your cleanser is wrong

  • Skin feels tight and needs moisturiser urgently rather than eventually.
  • You are oilier by midday than you were before you changed cleanser.
  • Small, rough bumps appear along the jaw and hairline, which often means residue.
  • Stinging, flushing or a rash-like reaction, which means stop, not persevere.

In short

  • Judge a cleanser fifteen minutes after rinsing, not while your face is still wet.
  • Match the cleanser to how your skin behaves now, not to how it behaved two years ago.
  • Skin changes with season and climate, so a cleanser that worked in December may not suit March.
  • Give a new cleanser two weeks before deciding, unless it stings.
