Skincare for Kampala heat and humidity

Most skincare advice online is written for a climate none of us live in. Ours needs fewer layers and better sun protection.

The Beauty Crib Editors 7 August 2026 6 minute read

Uganda sits on the equator at altitude, which is a particular combination: strong ultraviolet light year round, warm days, and humidity that rises sharply in the rainy seasons. Skin behaves differently here than it does in the places most tutorials are filmed.

Two practical consequences. Products absorb more slowly when the air is already holding moisture, so heavy layers sit on the skin and mix with sweat. And ultraviolet exposure is high on cloudy days, so sun protection is not a dry-season decision.

Fewer steps, better chosen

A ten-step routine in this climate usually turns into pilling, shine and congestion. Four steps in the morning and four at night are plenty for most people: cleanse, treat, moisturise, protect.

  • Morning: gentle cleanse, a lightweight hydrating layer, a gel or fluid moisturiser, then sunscreen.
  • Evening: cleansing oil if you wore sunscreen or makeup, a second gentle cleanse, your active, then moisturiser.
  • Swap heavy creams for gel textures in the hotter months and save richer formulas for skin that genuinely needs them.

Sunscreen is the whole routine

Deeper skin tones have more natural protection against burning, and this is the source of a persistent myth that sunscreen is optional. It is not. Ultraviolet exposure drives hyperpigmentation and uneven tone, which is the concern that brings more people to a skincare counter here than any other.

Look for broad spectrum SPF 30 or higher in a texture you will actually wear every day. Modern Korean and Japanese formulas tend to be lighter and leave less of a white cast, which matters when the alternative is not wearing any.

The dark marks people spend the most money treating are largely a sun protection problem.

Controlling shine without stripping

Midday shine is the most common complaint, and the most commonly mishandled. Washing more often, with something stronger, tends to make it worse: skin compensates for aggressive cleansing by producing more oil.

A better approach is a mild salicylic acid or green tea cleanser used twice a day, a light moisturiser that does not tempt you to skip it, and blotting rather than rewashing at lunchtime.

Rainy season adjustments

When humidity climbs, most people can drop one layer and stay comfortable. Move a rich cream to night only, keep the hydrating layer, and let sunscreen do the sealing during the day. When the dry, dusty weeks arrive, reverse it: skin will ask for more, and there is no prize for refusing.

In short

  • Wear broad spectrum sunscreen daily, including cloudy days.
  • Prefer gel and fluid textures in hot, humid weeks.
  • Do not fight oil by cleansing harder. Fight it by cleansing consistently and gently.
  • Adjust your routine by season, not by trend.
