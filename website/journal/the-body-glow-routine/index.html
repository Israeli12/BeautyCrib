The body-glow routine

The skin below your jaw has the same needs as the skin above it, and a fraction of the attention.

The Beauty Crib Editors 18 June 2026 5 minute read

Body skin is thicker, drier and more exposed to friction than facial skin, and it is usually washed with something far harsher. That combination produces the things people ask about most: rough upper arms, dark patches at the knees and elbows, breakouts across the back and shoulders, and legs that look dull no matter how much lotion goes on.

Wash less aggressively

Very hot water and strongly perfumed soap strip the body's barrier just as effectively as a harsh face wash strips the face. Lukewarm water and a gentler cleanser change how skin feels within a week. If you break out across the back and shoulders, a benzoyl peroxide wash used two or three times a week is worth trying, left on for a minute before rinsing.

Exfoliate on a schedule, not a whim

Twice a week is enough. Chemical exfoliation with lactic or salicylic acid handles dullness and bumpy texture more evenly than a scrub, and it does not create the micro-scratches that make dark marks worse on deeper skin tones.

Scrubbing dark patches harder is the fastest way to keep them.

Moisturise while damp

The single most effective change: apply lotion or oil within three minutes of leaving the shower, while skin still holds water. You are sealing moisture in rather than adding it from scratch. Knees, elbows, ankles and heels take a second pass.

Protect what you have worked on

Sun exposure on the arms, chest and legs drives the same uneven tone that people treat on the face. If those areas are uncovered during the day, they need the same protection.

In short

  • Lukewarm water, gentler wash, no aggressive scrubbing.
  • Exfoliate chemically, twice a week, rather than scrubbing daily.
  • Moisturise within three minutes of showering, while skin is still damp.
  • Protect exposed skin from the sun to keep tone even.

Questions people ask
